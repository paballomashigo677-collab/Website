IT Policies & Standard Operating Procedures

Comprehensive IT governance and operational guidelines

1. Reasons for SOPs

Within the ICT department, Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) guarantee efficiency, accountability, and consistency. They offer a framework for managing IT operations and incidents, assist employees in understanding their roles, and ensure policy compliance. SOPs also enhance service delivery, foster a culture of best practices, and lower risks during catastrophe recovery.

2. IT Department Staff Structure

IT Position Task
1. Chief Information Officer (CIO) They are responsible for overlooking and fact checking strategies and operations that take place then takes it over to the CEO.
2. IT Manager The daily operations that are done by the IT team collectively are managed and all changes are reported to the CIO.
3. System Administrators Hardware that is on the premises is maintained and updated as much as it is required. This includes servers, networks and any infrastructure. They report to the IT manager.
4. IT security officer Security policies are created, implemented and kept so that they are exercised. Any concerns are reported to the IT Manager.
5. Help desk They provide services and support for end-users. They report to the IT Manager.
6. Developers Software applications are implemented and monitored for the business. They report to the IT Manager.

3. IT Security Policy Guidelines

  • Use unique passwords that cannot be easily attempted and must be changed on a regular basis
  • Severe security measures need to take place for sensitive systems, and this includes:
    • Department card access
    • Biometrics
  • When there are devices that are not in use at the time in the facility, they need to be always locked to avoid any malicious activity
  • Activities or resources required by a certain department should only be accessed by that department. This is for accessing data and systems
  • Encryption of every file is important before any transition and storage for privacy reasons
  • If there any suspected malicious activity or security accident taking place, then it is required to report to the IT Officer.
  • Any unsupervised or illegal installation of software is prohibited
  • Anti-malware software needs to be working and always updating
  • For any remote use and access, the company is required to be alerted and approved by use of a VPN
  • Security measures like backups are required to be regularly implemented and verified.

Password Policy

  • Must contain uppercase and lowercase letters

Disaster Recovery Plan

Comprehensive strategy for business continuity and system recovery

Critical Priority: This disaster recovery plan ensures Games for Learning can maintain operations during unexpected disruptions and quickly recover from catastrophic events.

Disaster Recovery Objectives

  • RTO (Recovery Time Objective): 4 hours
  • RPO (Recovery Point Objective): 1 hour
  • Critical Systems Priority: High
  • Data Backup Frequency: Every 6 hours
  • Backup Retention: 30 days

Disaster Types Covered

  • Hardware failure (servers, switches, storage)
  • Network connectivity loss
  • Power outages
  • Cyber attacks (ransomware, DDoS)
  • Natural disasters (fire, flood)
  • Data corruption or loss

Backup Strategy

Backup Type Frequency Location Retention
Full Backup Weekly (Sunday 2 AM) Site A & Site B servers 4 weeks
Incremental Backup Every 6 hours Site A & Site B servers 7 days
Cloud Backup Daily (Midnight) Off-site cloud storage 90 days
Critical Systems Real-time replication Both sites + cloud 30 days

Recovery Procedures

Phase 1: Initial Response (0-30 minutes)

  • Identify and classify the disaster
  • Activate disaster recovery team
  • Notify key stakeholders and management
  • Assess extent of damage and impact
  • Initiate communication protocols

Phase 2: System Assessment (30-60 minutes)

  • Evaluate critical systems status
  • Determine recovery priority order
  • Identify required resources and personnel
  • Verify backup integrity and availability
  • Document all findings and actions taken

Phase 3: Recovery Execution (1-4 hours)

  • Restore critical systems from backups
  • Activate failover systems if available
  • Reconfigure network connectivity
  • Test restored systems functionality
  • Gradually bring users back online

Phase 4: Validation & Monitoring (4-8 hours)

  • Verify all systems operational
  • Confirm data integrity and completeness
  • Monitor system performance closely
  • Provide user support and assistance
  • Document recovery process and lessons learned

  • Redirect all traffic to Site B network
  • Remote access via VPN for affected users
  • Cloud-based collaboration tools activation

Site B Failure Scenarios:

  • Continue operations from Site A
  • Temporary workstation setup at Site A
  • Remote work arrangements for Site B staff
  • Access to cloud backups and resources
